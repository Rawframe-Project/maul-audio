# Voice processing

The four processors in `maul-audio/voice.h` are for programs that capture speech where the platform offers no processing, or offers processing a program cannot rely on. Each is a standalone object with no context, works on any frame count with results that do not depend on how the frames are cut, and allocates only when it is made. They share small parts ([voice_dsp.h](../../src/voice_dsp.h): biquads, levels) and the detector's analysis ([vad_core.h](../../src/vad_core.h)).

The detector, gain control and suppressor follow the designs WebRTC's processing uses; the echo canceller was measured against Speex's on the AEC Challenge's synthetic clips. The source files name the papers each algorithm comes from.

## Frame independence

Every processor works in fixed frames internally (a hundredth of the rate's frames, rounded down, so 10 ms or a hair less at a rate that is not a multiple of 100; or the echo canceller's blocks) and keeps what does not complete a frame for the next call. A time a def gives in seconds, such as the detector's hangover or the gain control's fastest change, counts frames at their real length. A host may therefore call it with whatever its stream delivers, and the output is the same as with any other cut. The tests feed the same input in different cuts and compare.

## The voice detector

[vad_core.c](../../src/vad_core.c) follows the classic band-energy design: six band levels from band-pass filters after a high-pass, each against its own noise floor, the minimum of its smoothed level over 1.5 s. A frame is speech when the weighted mean of the bands' margins over their floors passes a global threshold, or when one band above 250 Hz passes a local one (rumble lives below 250 Hz, and voice is never only there). The decision turns on after an onset and stays on for the hangover. `aggressiveness` raises the thresholds. The detector listens to the channels' mean.

## The gain control

[gain_control.c](../../src/gain_control.c) is an adaptive digital controller. At each 10 ms frame the detector's analysis says how confident it is of speech; the speech level is a weighted average over confident frames, reliable after twelve in a row. That level sets a target gain, capped so the noise floor stays under its ceiling, and the gain moves toward it by a bounded step per second, rising only after twelve confident frames in a row, so it never pumps up the noise between words. The gain ramps linearly across the next frame, and a peak limiter keeps every sample under -1 dBFS. It adds no delay: the gain follows what the frames before showed.

## The noise suppressor

[noise_suppressor.c](../../src/noise_suppressor.c) runs a high-pass biquad first, then frames of 20 ms under a square-root Hann window, 10 ms apart. The noise spectrum follows Gerkmann and Hendriks' unbiased MMSE estimate under a speech presence probability, which tracks changing noise within tens of milliseconds where a minimum-statistics search takes a second. Each frequency's gain is Cohen's optimally modified log-spectral amplitude estimate under that probability, floored at `floorDb`. The channels' mean power sets one gain per frequency for all channels, so a stereo image keeps its place. The output lags the input by two hops, 20 ms: a hop is whole only once the frame that covers it and the next hop has been weighed, and since the call works in place on any count of frames, each sample goes out as a new one comes in. A 10 ms lag would take a 10 ms frame every 5 ms, which halves the frequency resolution, doubles the transforms and leaves the noise estimate's constants, measured for 10 ms hops, to be measured again.

## The echo canceller

The echo canceller has a linear stage and a suppressor ([echo_canceller.c](../../src/echo_canceller.c)):

- **Before**: the capture through a DC notch, both signals through a pre-emphasis, whose coefficients are set in hertz so they mean the same at any rate.
- **The linear stage** ([echo_filter.h](../../src/echo_filter.h)): a multidelay block frequency-domain adaptive filter (Soo and Pang) over `tailTime` of echo path. Its step size per frequency follows the leakage of the echo into the error (Valin), proportionate across partitions. Until it has adapted, it steps at Speex's start rate, min(0.25 Sxx / See, 0.25), with the render's energy Sxx taken times the echo's gain over the render: the capture's energy over the render's, at least 1, falling at once and rising at most 10 dB a second. The error's energy See slows the step while a near end speaks, and an echo louder than the render is not taken for one: one 18 dB louder is learnt in about 2 s, against 0.8 s at the render's level. Against the render's energy alone, as in Speex, an echo g times louder takes g^2 times as long (30 s at 18 dB); WebRTC AEC3's coarse filter, whose rate ignores the error's level, learns any gain at one pace. The start ends, and the filter counts as adapted, once the start rate's sum passes the partitions and the background's error has been 3 dB or more under the capture for 0.1 s running. A block meets that by chance only when the capture correlates with the estimate by 1 / sqrt(2) or more; for a capture independent of the render, the chance that 0.1 s of blocks all do is under 1e-130 an hour for white noise, and under 1e-12 an hour for a capture of only 8 degrees of freedom a block (the bound is in `echo_filter.c`). A capture that follows the render, a tone at its own frequency, is an echo to any filter and is learnt as one. A capture that holds none of the render (muted, or through a headset) takes the full start rate against an error of nothing but leaves that error no lower than the capture, so the start goes on until an echo is heard. It must: the adapted rates follow the echo estimate, and from an estimate of nothing they learn an echo at about a ten-thousandth of the start's pace. Two filters run: a background one adapts, a foreground one produces the output, and a statistical test hands the background's coefficients over when it does better or takes them back when it diverges. Double talk (both ends speaking) is what diverges a single adaptive filter; this keeps the output from following it. Once adapted, a block whose capture is under 0.01 of the foreground estimate's energy (20 dB) is quiet, and its output is the capture itself, nothing subtracted: a capture c less an estimate y ten times its amplitude leaves at least (|y| - |c|)^2, 0.81 of the estimate and 19 dB over the capture, whatever their correlation. A real path does not lose 20 dB from one block to the next, but a mute or a switch of device does. A quiet block correlated with the estimate by at most 0.5 is absent; a quieter echo of the same path follows the estimate, correlated near 1, where a capture of zeros correlates with it by 0 and noise or a near end independent of it by about 1 / sqrt(n) over n samples (a block that passes 0.5 by chance is taken as present for what is learnt). The background takes no step for the first second of absence running, and the foreground takes the background only when the background's error is 3 dB under the capture. An absence runs on through a quiet block that passes the correlation by chance, and past its first second the foreground takes the background on the same condition in such a block too: by then the background has stepped toward whatever the capture holds, and a faint narrowband capture passes 0.5 in about one block in six. So a mute in a call is heard as silence and keeps the learnt path, and the echo after it is taken out as before it; subtracting the estimate from a silent capture would send the echo's estimate out, inverted. A path that only turns quieter, with no near end over it, is learnt as any change is, and one both quieter and moved, which the estimate does not follow, is learnt once the second's hold ends; until the estimate falls to within 20 dB of the capture, the capture comes through as it is. The step's products are formed in double, since each bin of the unscaled transforms may pass 2^64 for loud input, and each weight is kept within 2^13 a part, the most gain the filter models in a bin of one partition (78 dB a partition): with it, every float the canceller stores stays within the float range for samples within 2^64 of zero (the bound's chain is in `echo_filter.c`).
- **The suppressor** ([echo_suppressor.h](../../src/echo_suppressor.h)): one gain per frequency against the noise and the residual echo the filter leaves, the residual estimated from the echo estimate's power and the leakage of its band, speech presence from smoothed per-band SNRs, and a log-spectral amplitude gain floored between a noise floor and an echo floor.

Blocks are the smallest power of two of frames lasting 8 ms or more: 128 at 16 kHz, 512 at 48 kHz. Shorter blocks at 48 kHz cost the suppressor more frequency resolution than the filter gained in tracking. The output lags the input by two blocks.

The canceller is not given the delay between what was played and what the microphone hears: the host aligns the two from the streams' clocks, and the filter's length absorbs what is left. It replaces the noise suppressor; running both suppresses twice.

## Real-time cost

At 48 kHz on one desktop core, noise suppression costs about 41 microseconds per 10 ms of mono audio and echo cancellation about 76 (see the README's cost table, from `bench/baseline.txt`). The transforms are the library's real FFT ([real_fft.h](../../src/real_fft.h)), whose tables live in memory the processor allocated, so a transform allocates nothing.
